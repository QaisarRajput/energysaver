# EV owners: the cheapest, greenest hours to charge in the UK

A typical EV adds 10–30 kWh of electricity demand per day when driven regularly. That's as much as the rest of your house combined — and it's almost entirely flexible. You just need to charge before the next morning.

## When to charge

**Best hours (most nights):** 00:30–05:30 UK time.

During this window:
- Grid demand is at its lowest.
- Gas peaker plants have largely cycled off.
- Wind power (which doesn't stop at night) makes up a higher share of generation.
- On Agile, prices in this window are typically **5–12p/kWh** vs 30p+ at peak.

**Worst hours:** 17:00–21:00 — the evening peak. Charging then costs 2–4× more on a TOU tariff and is significantly dirtier.

## A typical saving

Charging a 60 kWh battery from 20% to 80% = 36 kWh.

| Scenario | Rate | Cost | CO₂ |
|----------|------|------|-----|
| Evening peak (flat) | 24.5p | £8.82 | ~10.1 kg |
| Overnight (Octopus Go) | 9p | **£3.24** | ~3.5 kg |
| Overnight (Agile, good night) | 7p | **£2.52** | ~3.5 kg |

**Saving: £5–£6 per full charge.** For a household charging 4× per week that's **£1,000–£1,200/year** in avoided peak-rate electricity.

## Which tariff to use?

**Octopus Go:** fixed 9p/kWh from 00:30–05:30. Simple, predictable, great for EV owners who just set a charge timer and forget it.

**Octopus Agile:** variable, sometimes as low as 0–2p (or negative). Optimal for EV owners with a smart charger or home energy management system that can automatically find the cheapest window each night.

**Economy 7:** off-peak from ~00:30–07:30. Works with an in-car timer, but the rate (~15p) is higher than Go or Agile's best hours.

## How to automate it

Most EVs have a scheduled charging option in their app or on the in-car screen. Set it to start charging at 00:30 and stop when full. If you're on Agile, a smart home plugin or your charger's app can pick the cheapest slots automatically.
